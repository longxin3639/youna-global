### 4.3 Content gaps - our opportunity (ranked by value)

1. **The sample-to-mass-production gap / BOM lock.** Competitor #6 gets closest with "production samples not shelf samples", and #3 notes that a large share of its inspection failures traced to undisclosed component substitution. Nobody explains the countermeasure: a locked BOM with named component part numbers, a signed golden sample retained by both parties, and a written change-notification clause. **This is the highest-value gap and should be the article's spine.**
2. **After-sales and DOA planning.** Zero of ten competitors cover it. Searching for it returns component-distributor pages, not buyer guidance. Electronics differ from apparel precisely because units fail in the field. Cover: negotiating a DOA replacement allowance, ordering a spare-parts and accessories buffer with the main run, and who owns firmware/app maintenance for connected products.
3. **A real cross-category FOB price and MOQ ladder.** Competitors describe prices qualitatively ("competitive", "40 percent lower"). None publish a verifiable table. We can, from live product pages. **This is the moat.**
4. **Battery as a logistics constraint, not just a compliance box.** Competitors list UN38.3 as a certificate. None explain that a battery changes your *shipping options*: DG-certified forwarder required, restricted air options, state-of-charge limits, and the fact that a UN38.3 report plus MSDS plus a transport identification report must all match the exact model number or the forwarder rejects the booking. This adds real lead time.
5. **Certificate verification as a 10-minute procedure.** Most say "ask for a test report". Only one mentions the FCC ID lookup. Nobody gives the full check: FCC ID database lookup, CE Declaration of Conformity is self-declared and is not a test report, lab report must name the exact model and be from an accredited lab, and a certificate for a sibling model does not cover your unit.
6. **Category risk tiering.** No competitor segments electronics by risk. An original 4-tier framework (passive accessories / battery-containing / RF-wireless / mains-powered) is genuinely useful, highly linkable, and a strong featured-snippet candidate.
7. **Non-US destination markets.** Most guides are US-first. Youna serves EU, UK, Gulf, Africa, SEA, LatAm buyers. Even brief but accurate coverage differentiates.
8. **Honest treatment of 2026 tariff volatility.** Competitors state single numbers that contradict each other. A calibrated "here is the range, here is why it moves, verify with your broker" builds more trust than false precision.

### 4.4 Differentiation statement

> Every other guide tells you how to *find* an electronics supplier. This one tells you how to stop the order from drifting between the sample you approved and the pallet that ships - with real 2026 factory prices, real MOQs, and the five specific traps that cost buyers money.

## 9. Fact-Check and Compliance Notes (must handle carefully)

### 9.1 Tariffs - CONFLICTING SOURCES, DO NOT STATE A SINGLE NUMBER

Live sources retrieved on 2026-07-31 actively contradict each other:

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| tariff-check.com | Consumer electronics under List 4A at 7.5 percent Section 301 plus a 15 percent Section 122 surcharge |
| china-electronics.com | Most consumer electronics List 3 at 7.5 percent; laptops and smartphones still exempt from Section 301; combined 17.5 - 35 percent |
| tariffstool.com | 10 percent Section 122 plus 25 percent Section 301 on most electronics; approx 35 percent effective |
| newbuyingagent.com | Effective 35 - 85 percent depending on category |
| customsbrokerindex.com | Section 301 on electronics at 25 percent or higher, some categories raised to 35 percent |

They also disagree on when de minimis ended for China (variously May 2025, February 4 2026, February 25 2026), and several reference a Section 122 measure with a stated mid-2026 expiry plus active litigation.

**Instruction to writer:** state a **range** (most consumer electronics categories land somewhere in the broad 10 - 35 percent-plus band depending on HTS code), state plainly that the rules changed several times across 2025 - 2026 and remain subject to litigation and expiry dates, and direct the reader to confirm their specific HTS code with a licensed customs broker. Link to `blog-import-china-tariff-guide-2026.html`. **Do not** publish a single authoritative-sounding percentage. Do not cite specific case names or court outcomes.

### 9.2 De minimis

Safe to state: the USD 800 de minimis exemption no longer applies to China-origin goods, so small and sample shipments now attract duty and formal entry.
