Private Label Manufacturing in China: The 2026 Importer's Guide

Private label is how most consumer brands you see on Amazon, Shopify, and retail shelves are actually made. A Chinese factory produces the product; you put your brand on it. This guide explains how private label works in China in 2026, what it really costs, how to protect your brand, and the step-by-step process from idea to container.

01 What Private Label Manufacturing Actually Means

Private label manufacturing means a factory makes a product, and you sell it under your own brand name. The customer sees your logo, your packaging, and your story, not the factory's. The factory may also make the same or a similar product for other buyers, unless you negotiate exclusivity.

The term gets confused with two close relatives, so it is worth separating them clearly:

  • White label: a generic, off-the-shelf product you resell with almost no changes, often just a sticker or a plain label. Cheapest and fastest, but almost no differentiation. Everyone else can buy the exact same item.
  • Private label: you select an existing factory product as a base and customize it with your logo, packaging, colors, and brand identity. More differentiation than white label, far cheaper and faster than building from scratch.
  • OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer): you design the product from scratch with your own engineering specs, and the factory builds it to your drawings. Most expensive and slowest, but fully unique.
  • ODM (Original Design Manufacturer): the factory already has a design you can adopt and modify. This is the closest cousin to private label and the route most private label projects actually take.

Private label sits in the sweet spot:

White label is too generic. OEM is too slow and costly for a first product. Private label (often via ODM) gives you a branded, differentiated product with manageable MOQ and lead time. That is why it is the standard entry point for new importers.

02 Why China Is the Default Choice for Private Label

You can private label from Vietnam, India, or Turkey, but China remains the default for most importers, and the reason is structural, not just price.

The ecosystem is the product

China's advantage is not a single cheap factory. It is the whole chain in one place: raw materials, components, molding, printing, packaging, testing labs, and freight forwarders all within a short drive of each other. When you need to change a color, add a feature, or rush a re-order, that density is what saves weeks.

Clusters by category

03 Realistic MOQs for Private Label

Minimum order quantity is the number that decides whether a private label project is viable for you. Here is what to expect in 2026:

  • Simple accessories, basic goods, packaging-heavy items: often 100 to 500 pieces per design or color
  • Most private label categories: initial runs typically start at 200 to 1,000 units
  • Electronics or custom-molded products: usually 1,000 to 5,000 units because of tooling and setup
  • White label with minimal changes: can sometimes start as low as 50 to 100 units, but this is not true private label
MOQ red flag: If a factory quotes MOQ of 10 or 50 units for a heavily customized product, read the fine print. They are almost certainly offering white label with a logo print, not the custom-molded, differentiated product you imagine. Manage your expectations accordingly, or budget for the real MOQ.

Remember that MOQ rises the moment you add custom molding, custom colors with minimum paint batches, or bespoke packaging. A plain base product can have a low MOQ; your branded version of it usually does not. 04 What Private Label Really Costs in 2026

The biggest myth in private label is that the unit price is the cost. It is not. The real cost is the sum of samples, tooling, inventory, packaging, freight, duties, and marketing. Below are typical 2026 ranges from real projects, so you can sanity-check any quote you receive.

Branding add-ons (per unit)

  • Logo printing or engraving: about $0.05 to $0.30 per unit, minimal MOQ impact
  • Custom colors (Pantone-matched): about $0.10 to $0.50 per unit, may need a minimum batch
  • Custom retail packaging: about $0.30 to $2.00 per unit depending on material and complexity
  • Branded inserts (cards, manuals, warranty): about $0.05 to $0.20 per unit

Total launch budget

A lean launch typically runs 3,000 to 8,000 US dollars. That covers samples, first inventory, custom packaging, shipping and customs, a basic sales channel, and starter marketing. If your product needs custom tooling, budget 10,000 to 25,000 US dollars upfront, because the mold cost lands before you ship a single unit.

Budget 6 to 12 weeks from sample approval to goods at your warehouse on a first order.

Costs drop sharply from the second order: tooling is already paid, sampling is faster, and production slots are easier to book because you are a returning customer. Plan your cash flow around the first order being the expensive one.

Do not forget the costs that arrive after the factory. Freight, import duties, and hidden fees can erase a healthy unit margin if you ignore them. Run the full landed-cost math using our hidden costs guide and our 2026 tariff guide before you commit.

05 The Step-by-Step Private Label Process

Whether you go ODM, OEM, or hybrid, the workflow has the same skeleton. Here is exactly how we run it, and how you should run it if you go solo.

Step 1: Define your product spec. Write everything down before contacting anyone: target price, dimensions, materials, colors, packaging, compliance needs, and your landed-cost ceiling. A vague brief gets vague quotes. This step alone determines the quality of everything that follows.
Step 2: Shortlist suppliers, not just products. For ODM, search factory catalogs on Alibaba, Global Sources, and at trade shows like the Canton Fair. For OEM, look for factories with proven capability in your category. Our guide on telling traders from manufacturers helps you know who you are actually talking to.
Step 3: Send an RFQ and compare like-for-like. Send the same request for quotation to 5 to 8 factories. Compare unit price, tooling cost, MOQ, lead time, payment terms, and certifications, not just the bottom line. A quote that is 30 percent cheaper with a 12,000 dollar mold fee is not cheaper.
Step 4: Order samples, plural. Never approve a product on a single sample. Order pre-production samples from your top 2 to 3 factories, side by side, and test them like a customer would. This is the cheapest quality control you will ever buy. Our sample evaluation guide walks through the method.
Step 5: Lock down tooling and IP. If you pay for the mold, get mold ownership in the contract, in writing, in both English and Chinese. Register your trademark in China before you launch. Our supplier scam guide covers the traps here.
Step 6: Pilot production. Run a small first batch, 10 to 20 percent of your target order, and inspect it against your spec sheet line by line. Catching a tolerance problem on 200 units costs a weekend; catching it on 2,000 units costs a quarter.
Step 7: Pre-shipment inspection. Before the container leaves, have every carton checked against an AQL-based inspection: quantities, colors, logos, packaging, drop-test samples, carton markings. We do this on 100 percent of shipments. See our QC inspection checklist for what gets checked.
Step 8: Ship and clear customs. Choose your Incoterm, book freight, and handle duties. This is where 2026 logistics economics bite hardest, so build buffer into your timeline.
Step 9: Measure, iterate, reorder. Track your landed cost per unit against your actual margin, and feed that data back into Step 1. The brands that win treat every order as a data point, not a gamble.

06 Protecting Your Brand and IP

Your brand is the asset that compounds over time, so protecting it is not optional. The traps are specific to China, and ignoring them is the most expensive mistake new importers make.

Register your trademark in China first

China uses a first-to-file system. If someone else registers your brand name in China first, you can lose the right to use it there, even if you created it and sell under it elsewhere. The cost is modest, roughly 300 to 600 US dollars per class through a Chinese trademark agent. The cost of not doing it can be catastrophic: we have seen clients lose six figures when a squatter registered their name first.

Design patents for distinctive products

If your product has a unique look, a design patent filed in China can stop copycats in the Chinese market and in export markets that honor Chinese IP. This is worth pursuing for products where appearance is the differentiator.

The uncomfortable truth about NDAs

An NDA with a Chinese factory has limited enforceability and provides a false sense of security. Do not rely on it as your main protection. Instead, reduce what the factory knows: share only the specifications they need to manufacture, not your full product roadmap or customer pricing. Pair that with trademark and design registration, and you have real protection.

Copycat reality: No strategy makes copying impossible. Focus on building brand loyalty, strong reviews, and a unique customer experience as your long-term moat. IP registration slows copycats and gives you legal footing; brand strength is what actually wins.

07 Quality Control for Private Label

With private label, your brand name is on the product, so a defect is your reputation, not the factory's. Quality control is therefore more important than for a generic resale item.

Approve a golden sample

Before bulk production, get a sample with your branding applied and approve it as the reference standard. Every production unit is then measured against that golden sample. If the factory "improves" something during the run, the golden sample is your proof of what was agreed.

Stage your inspections

  • Pre-production check: verify materials and packaging components before production starts
  • In-line inspection: check at 40 to 60 percent production completion to catch issues early
  • Final random inspection: AQL 2.5 standard on the completed batch, verifying branding accuracy, packaging quality, and product function

Our 8-step factory audit checklist and our sample evaluation guide cover the details. For private label, pay special attention to logo placement, color accuracy, and packaging finish, because those are what the customer sees first.

Rule of thumb: a factory that resists inspection or cannot show test reports is a factory to walk away from. The savings on skipping QC disappear the moment a single bad batch reaches your customers.

08 The Most Common Private Label Mistakes

1. Skipping sample orders. Approving on a single photo or one sample is how subpar products reach customers. Order from multiple factories and test like a buyer.
2. Choosing the cheapest supplier. The lowest quote often means a material or process shortcut you pay for later in returns and reviews. Compare like-for-like, not just price.
3. Underestimating landed cost. A low unit price can become a negative margin once freight, duties, and fees are counted. Always run full landed-cost math first.
4. Neglecting trademark registration. Waiting until after launch to register your brand in China is a gamble you can lose. Register before your first deposit.
5. Ordering too much too soon. Big first orders create cash-flow problems and amplify any quality miss. Start with a pilot batch, prove the product, then scale.
6. Using unlicensed designs. Do not source products bearing licensed characters or brands you do not own. Customs seize them and platforms ban the listings.

10 Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between private label, white label, and OEM?

White label is a generic off-the-shelf product you resell with almost no changes. Private label means you pick an existing factory product and add your own logo, packaging, colors, and brand identity. OEM means you design the product from scratch and the factory builds it to your engineering specs. Private label sits in the middle: faster and cheaper than OEM, more differentiated than white label.

What is a realistic MOQ for private label in China?

For most categories, initial private label runs start around 200 to 1,000 units. Simple products such as accessories, basic electronics, or packaging-heavy goods can sometimes start at 100 to 500 pieces per design. If a factory quotes MOQ of 10 or 50 units for a heavily customized product, they are usually offering white label with minimal changes, not true private label.

How much does it cost to launch a private label brand from China in 2026?

A lean launch typically runs 3,000 to 8,000 US dollars: samples, first inventory, custom packaging, shipping, and basic marketing. If your product needs custom tooling such as a new mold, budget 10,000 to 25,000 US dollars upfront. Unit prices vary widely, but private label base products often land in the 6 to 15 US dollars per unit range at 500 to 1,000 MOQ, before freight and duties.

Should I register my trademark in China before ordering?

Yes. China uses a first-to-file trademark system. If someone else registers your brand name in China first, you can lose the right to use it there, even if you created it. Registering a trademark in China costs roughly 300 to 600 US dollars per class through a Chinese trademark agent, and it is one of the cheapest forms of protection you can buy before sending a deposit.

Do I need a sourcing agent for private label?

Not strictly, but a good agent earns their fee on private label more than almost any other product type. They vet factories, negotiate like-for-like quotes, manage sampling and pre-shipment inspection, and coordinate shipping. If you are new to importing or your product is complex, an agent reduces the chance of an expensive mistake on your first order.
