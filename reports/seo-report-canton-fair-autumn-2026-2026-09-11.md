3. FAQPage (recommended - justified)
   Yes, the FAQ section justifies FAQPage schema. Here is the exact test and why it passes:

## 6. Featured snippet capture

Three realistic snippet targets exist, all matching brief-identified intents. Two need tightening because their answer is currently split across two near-duplicate passages within the same page.

Target 1 - Paragraph snippet: "how many days do you need at the Canton Fair"
- Current passage: FAQ answer (line 179): "Plan on three full days for a single phase, four to five to cover one category deeply, and about a week if your products span two phases."
- Problem: the same answer also appears at line 59 ("Three full days is the realistic minimum for a single phase..."). Two near-identical answers dilute the snippet signal.
- Tighten: choose the FAQ answer as the canonical snippet block and trim to lead with the number in a self-contained first sentence:
  "Plan on three full days for a single phase. Budget four to five days to cover one category in depth, and about a week if your products span two phases."
  Then change the line 59 section to add detail (why, and how the changeover gaps fit) rather than restating the number.

Target 2 - List snippet: "what should you bring to the Canton Fair"
- Current passage: the "The daily field kit" bullets (lines 118 to 124) and the FAQ answer (line 175).
- Tighten: add a single lead sentence immediately before the bullet list that answers the question, then keep 4 to 6 tight bullets. Suggested lead: "For a full day on the floor, bring:" followed by the existing bullets. Rename the H3 from the declarative "The daily field kit" to the query-shaped "What to bring: the daily field kit" so the list maps cleanly to the search phrase.

Target 3 - List/definition snippet: "when is Canton Fair Autumn 2026"
- Current passage: the H3 "Key dates and the three phases" (line 27) plus the phase list (lines 31 to 35), and the FAQ answer (line 171).
- Tighten: keep the H3 but make it read "Key dates: the three phases in 2026", and ensure the first three bullets are plain "Phase X: <dates>, 2026" lines with no trailing commentary, so a crawler can lift them verbatim. The current list already does this well; just remove the thematic text after the dates from the first three bullets and move it to a following sentence.

## 8. Cannibalization review versus the two existing Canton Fair articles

Files reviewed: blog-canton-fair-guide.html (evergreen guide, spring + generic) and blog-canton-fair-2026-recap.html (post-event recap of the 139th). Verdict: the keyword allocation HOLDS, with two required fixes already listed as blocking items 1 and 2. The three pages are genuinely distinct by time orientation (pre-event vs evergreen vs post-event) and by core intent (plan vs evaluate vs recap). No primary keyword collides.

Overlaps still present, ranked:

1. Heading overlap (highest risk). A20 H3 "Questions that separate a real factory from a trading company" (line 130) vs Article 1 H2 "Questions That Separate Real Suppliers from Trading Companies" (line 155). Same intent, nearly the same words. A20 already links out to Article 1 here, so only the heading needs to change. Fix = blocking item 1.

2. FAQ duplication (moderate). A20 "How many days do you need at the Canton Fair?" duplicates Article 1's "How many days do I need at the Canton Fair?" across two different pages. Duplicate FAQ questions on two URLs can split the FAQ signal and cause thin duplication. Fix = blocking item 2. The other four A20 FAQ questions (dates, what to bring, visa, worth-it) do not collide with Article 1's FAQ set.

3. Post-fair section intent (moderate, acceptable). A20 H2 "After the Fair: Turning Cards into Orders" (with the 7-day follow-up rule) shares intent with Article 2 H2 "You Attended the Canton Fair - Now What?" (which cites a 7 to 10 day follow-up). Article 2 owns the post-fair, 139th-specific angle; A20's version is a generic how-to. Keeping A20's section short (it currently is) and linking to Article 2 in the conclusion (it does, line 193) is sufficient. Optional tidy: retitle the A20 H2 to "After the Fair: The 7-Day Follow-Up Rule" so it reads clearly as a how-to rather than a recap.

4. "Is it worth going" intent (low). A20 H2 "Decide Whether to Attend and Which Phase" plus FAQ "Is the Canton Fair worth it for importers?" touches Article 1 H2 "Do You Actually Need to Go?". Risk is low because A20 frames the decision around phase selection, which Article 1 does not. Keep the A20 FAQ answer focused on 2026 specifics to hold the separation.

5. Title and slug coexistence (low, no action). Article 1 uses "The Canton Fair Guide 2026"; A20 uses "Canton Fair Autumn 2026 ... Guide" and the slug contains "guide". Both share the tokens "Canton Fair" and "2026", but the "Autumn"/"140th" qualifiers separate them. No two pages share a primary keyword. Keeping the seasonal qualifier in A20's title (recommended in Section 3) preserves this separation.

Word-level phrase overlap to watch (present in both A20 and the existing articles, keep these brief in A20): "trading companies pretending to be factories", "the fair is overwhelming by design", and "the fair is a starting point, not the finish line" appear in the existing articles. A20 currently reuses only the concept, not the phrases, so it is clean. Do not import those exact sentences during the integration edit.

Conclusion: publish after fixing blocking items 1 through 4. The two cannibalization edits are small heading/answer rewrites, not structural changes. The remaining items are polish.
