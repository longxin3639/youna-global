The China Plus One Decision Is Complicated. Here's the Honest Take.

Every importer is asking the same question right now: should I stay with China, or split my orders? The answer depends on your product, your market, and who you have on the ground.

The Challenges You're Facing

  • Tariff uncertainty making China costs unpredictable
  • Vietnam still sources 60%+ of components from China — you're not escaping the dependency, just adding a middleman
  • India ramp-up is real but slow — BIS certification, compliance overhead, and management bandwidth add months to your timeline
  • Without someone on the ground, you can't verify which factories in any country are actually reliable
  • Dual-sourcing sounds smart in theory but doubles your management overhead and negotiation complexity

How Karsa Solves This

  • Local presence in Guangdong — visits factories, verifies licenses, checks real production capacity
  • Factory vetting in Vietnam and India too — if you want to diversify, we research alternatives for you
  • HS code optimization — we classify your products to minimize duty within legal boundaries
  • Consolidated shipping — multiple suppliers, one shipment, dramatically lower per-unit logistics cost
  • Honest advice — if Vietnam or India genuinely makes sense for your product, we'll tell you. If China is still your best option, we'll tell you that too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers to the questions every importer is asking right now.

Is China still the best sourcing option despite tariffs?
For most importers, yes. China's full supply chain ecosystem, speed-to-production, and OEM capabilities remain unmatched. Vietnam and India still depend heavily on Chinese components for anything beyond basic assembly. A local sourcing agent can help you legally optimize landed costs through bulk pricing, HS code optimization, and consolidated shipping.
Can I use China as my primary source and Vietnam as a backup?
Absolutely — and many smart importers do exactly this. The key is not treating Vietnam as a cost-saving measure (it's not, when you factor in component sourcing from China, logistics, and management overhead), but as a risk hedge for specific product lines. A sourcing agent can help you evaluate which products suit dual-sourcing and negotiate with factories in both countries.
What does a sourcing agent actually do that I cannot do myself?
Three things: 1) On-the-ground factory vetting — visiting factories, verifying business licenses, checking production capacity. 2) Quality control — inspecting goods before shipment so you don't receive defective products. 3) Consolidation — combining orders from multiple suppliers into one shipment, dramatically reducing your per-unit shipping cost. Without a local agent, you rely on photos and trust; with one, you have verified eyes on the ground.
How do your fees work?
We charge 3% commission on the order value for order management (sourcing, negotiation, QC, shipping coordination). Factory visits for supplier vetting are $150/day (you cover travel and accommodation). Initial consultation is free. There are no hidden fees — we quote you an all-in landed cost before you commit to any order.
