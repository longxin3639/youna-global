## 1. The Main Types of GPS Trackers (and Which You Actually Need)

"GPS tracker" is a catch-all. The type decides the price, the battery, the certification, and whether it will even work where you sell.

- **Vehicle trackers** — hardwired (9–90V) or OBD plug-in. Used for fleet, car theft recovery, motorcycle. Often include remote engine cut-off and geofencing.
- **Asset trackers** — magnetic, rugged, long-battery (some 3000–10000mAh, 2–3 years standby). For containers, equipment, rental assets. IP65/IP67 waterproof.
- **Pet trackers** — collars or tags, usually 4G with a SIM. Lighter, smaller, often IP67. Price band $23–33 at factory.
- **Personal / wearable** — kids, elderly, lone workers. Pendants, watches, bracelets with SOS buttons.
- **Item / Bluetooth trackers** — AirTag-style. Short Bluetooth range (~100m) that leverages a phone network (e.g. Find My) for long-range. Cheapest to make ($4–9).
- **Dashcams / AI video** — 4G dashcams with ADAS/DMS for commercial fleets. Higher ticket, higher complexity.

If you're reselling to general consumers, the item/Bluetooth and pet categories move fastest. If you serve fleets or logistics, vehicle and asset are your lanes.

## 4. The Certification Maze (by Destination Market)

This is where most first-time importers stall. You can't just ship; the device must be legally marketable.

- **United States — FCC + FCC ID.** The FCC ID is the big one: it's the government filing proving the radio complies. For cellular trackers used on carrier networks, **PTCRB** certification (AT&T/T-Mobile/Verizon) is often required by the carrier. Without FCC ID, US customs and Amazon will block you.
- **European Union — CE RED** (Radio Equipment Directive). Plus RoHS and REACH (hazardous-substance restrictions) almost everywhere.
- **Canada — IC** (Industry Canada) certification.
- **Brazil — ANATEL.** Often overlooked; many "global" trackers aren't ANATEL-approved.
- **Factory quality — ISO 9001** is a baseline signal, not a product cert.

Rule of thumb: ask the supplier for the **actual cert files (PDFs with certificate numbers)**, not a screenshot. A real FCC ID can be looked up. A fuzzy "we are certified" claim cannot.

### Certification Requirements at a Glance

| Market | Must-have marks | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|
| United States | FCC ID (radio), PTCRB for carrier cellular | Cloned IMEI = blocked; Amazon requires FCC |
| European Union | CE RED, RoHS, REACH | 2G/3G sunset rolling out country by country |
| Canada | IC (Industry Canada) | Similar framework to FCC |
| Brazil | ANATEL | Often missing on "global" trackers |
| Australia | RCM | Smaller market, still mandatory |

Get the PDFs, not screenshots. A certificate number that can't be verified is not a certificate — and your retailer or platform will verify it before you ever ship.

## 5. The 2G / 3G Shutdown Warning (Read This Before You Order)

The single most expensive mistake in GPS sourcing right now is buying a 2G or 3G device for a market that no longer supports those networks.

- In the **US**, AT&T and Verizon discontinued 2G and 3G years ago; T-Mobile's legacy 2G/3G is being wound down. A "GSM tracker" spec often means 2G — and it will not register on US towers.
- In the **EU**, 2G/3G sunset is rolling out country by country, with full phase-out planned this decade.
- **What to specify instead:** **4G LTE**, ideally **Cat M1 / NB-IoT** for the US (these are the bands carriers keep alive for IoT). At minimum, confirm the module supports the LTE bands of your target country.

When a supplier says "4G," verify the **module model** (Quectel, u-blox, SIMCom are common) and the bands. "4G" printed on a listing is not the same as "4G bands that work where you sell."

## 6. Spec Checklist Before You Order

Print this and send it to every supplier:

1. **Network — which 4G bands?** For the US, insist on Cat M1 / NB-IoT; these are the IoT bands carriers keep alive. A tracker that only does European LTE bands is a paperweight in Ohio.
2. **Positioning — GPS alone, or multi-constellation?** GPS + GLONASS + BeiDou locks faster and holds signal in cities and under foliage. Single-GPS units drift and drop.
3. **Battery — real mAh and standby, not "up to."** A 5000mAh asset tracker may claim "3-year standby" in a lab; ask for the test condition. Pet and personal units trade battery for size.
4. **Ingress protection — IP65/IP67 for anything outdoors.** Asset and pet trackers live in rain, dust, and vibration. IP67 means brief immersion; IP65 means water jets. Indoor-only item trackers can skip this.
5. **Platform & fees — free for life, or subscription?** This is the quiet margin killer. A $15 tracker that needs $5/month per device flips your unit economics. Get it in writing before you commit.
6. **App language & data residency.** English app for Western customers; ask where the server sits (GDPR and privacy exposure if it's an unknown overseas host).
7. **IMEI — legal and unique.** Cloned IMEIs get blocked by carriers and can pull your whole shipment. Ask for the IMEI range allocation proof.
8. **Warranty — 1–2 years is the norm** for reputable makers. Anything shorter is a quality signal in itself.

## 7. The Find My / MFi Licensing Note (Honest Version)

A lot of "AirTag alternative" trackers are marketed as "Find My compatible." Here's the nuance most guides skip: genuine access to Apple's Find My network requires the **Apple MFi Find My license**. Unlicensed clones that claim compatibility may not actually work with the real network and carry takedown risk on Amazon and Apple's ecosystem.

If you plan to market a tracker as "Find My," confirm your supplier **holds the MFi Find My license** and can show it. If they can't, position it as a Bluetooth item-finder on its own app instead — safer and still sellable.

## 9. Red Flags & Scams

- Price far below the table in Section 2 → usually 2G hardware or a clone with cloned IMEIs.
- "We are certified" with no PDF cert file → walk away.
- Refuses a video call of the production floor → likely a trading desk.
- Demands 100% prepayment on a first order → standard red flag (see our [avoid-scams guide](https://www.youna-global.com/blog/blog-avoid-china-supplier-scams.html)).
- Vague on network bands when you ask "which 4G bands?" → they don't know, which means they don't build to spec.

### The most common first-order mistakes
Paying 100% upfront to a stranger. Trusting a screenshot instead of a verifiable cert number. Assuming "4G" means "works in my country." Skipping the sample because the price is too good. Ordering 2G hardware for a market that already shut 2G down. Every one of these is avoidable, and every one of them is covered in the sections above — this guide is built so you can't miss them. If you only remember one line: **verify the module model and the cert PDF before you pay, not after.**
